## 1. Definitions, theorem, and parameter order

Let m,K be positive integers. For positive rational weights W=(w₀,w₁,…,w_m), define P_W(H) to be the complex vector space spanned by monomials

    Y^h X₁^{α₁}⋯X_m^{α_m},    h,α_i≥0,
    w₀h+∑_{i=1}^m w_iα_i≤H.

For positive rational weights V=(v₀,v₁,…,v_m), let M_V(H) be the monomial ideal of C[[t,u₁,…,u_m]] generated by monomials of V-weight at least H, and write

    J_V(H)=C[[t,u₁,…,u_m]]/M_V(H).

Thus the source inequality is inclusive and the retained target inequality is strict. Positive weights make the target finite-dimensional.

### Theorem A

Fix m,K,w₀,v₀ and 0<θ<1, with w₀,v₀,θ rational and

    λ=K(w₀/v₀)θ^m<1.                                      (1.1)

There are successive lower bounds for rational w₁,…,w_m, each depending only on m,K,w₀,v₀,θ and the preceding weights, such that the following holds. Put

    v_i=w_i/θ    for i>0.

For every ordered collection of centers

    a_j=(y_j,c_{j1},…,c_{jm}),     1≤j≤K,

with nonzero pairwise distinct complex numbers y_j and arbitrary complex c_ji, there is a positive integer R depending only on W,V such that, for every sufficiently large integer n, the map

    P_W(nR) → ⊕_{j=1}^K J_V(nR),
    P ↦ (P(y_j(1+t),c_{j1}+u₁+log(1+t),…,
                       c_{jm}+u_m+log(1+t)))_j              (1.2)

is surjective. The lower bound on n is permitted to depend on the centers.

The logarithm throughout (1.2) is the formal series at t=0. No branch choice away from that neighborhood is involved.

### Explicit admissible weight bounds

Choose rational σ>0 such that

    (1+3σ)^{m+1}λ<1,       (1+σ)θ<1.                       (1.3)

Put

    L₀=m+2,
    C*=max_{1≤k≤m} k!(L₀/σ)^k,
    Q=C*θ^{−m} max(1,v₀)/min(1,w₀).

It suffices to choose successively

    w_i>max(1,Q∏_{1≤j<i}w_j).                              (1.4)

These choices precede the centers. Later auxiliary polynomials and algebraic components may depend on the centers and on their degree; the constants in (1.4) do not.

For equal-cardinality subsets A,B of {0,…,m}, of size 1 through m, suppose the largest positive index in A△B is i∈A. Common positive indices contribute θ to (∏_A w)/(∏_B v); lower denominator indices contribute at worst (∏_{j<i}w_j)^{−1}; other numerator factors are at least one. The index-zero factor is at least min(1,w₀)/max(1,v₀). Therefore

    (∏_{a∈A}w_a)/(∏_{b∈B}v_b)
       ≥ θ^m w_i min(1,w₀)/(max(1,v₀)∏_{j<i}w_j)>C*.      (1.5)

This inequality holds simultaneously for every such pair of index sets.

## 2. A local transverse-multiplicity bound

We first prove the local estimate that converts high differential vanishing into a comparison of normal bases.

### Lemma 2.1

Give the coordinates x₁,…,x_d of affine space positive rational weights ρ_i. On an open set, let commuting holomorphic vector fields D₁,…,D_d form a frame, and give them positive rational costs κ_i. Let f₁,…,f_r be polynomials of weighted degree at most N>0. Let Z be an irreducible codimension-k component of their common zero set, with k≥1. Suppose

    (D^γ f_l)|_Z=0 whenever ∑κ_iγ_i<εN.                   (2.1)

Choose a generic smooth point where both of the following exist:
- a coordinate normal basis A, meaning the images of ∂/∂x_a, a∈A, form a basis of the ambient tangent space modulo T_Z;
- a D-normal basis B with the analogous property.

Both sets have size k. Then

    ∏_{a∈A}ρ_a≤k!ε^{−k}∏_{b∈B}κ_b.                      (2.2)

#### Proof: flow coordinates and the slice

Choose the point away from every other irreducible component of the common zero set. A finite intersection of the required nonempty generic open loci is nonempty. Take holomorphic coordinates z on the smooth Z and flow off Z in the commuting B directions:

    Φ(z,b)=exp(b₁D_{B₁})⋯exp(b_kD_{B_k})(z).

Its derivative at b=0 is an isomorphism because the selected directions complement T_Z. The holomorphic inverse function theorem gives ambient coordinates (z,b), with Z={b=0} and D_{B_i}=∂/∂b_i. Only local germs are used. In the later application the flows are explicit: D₀ sends (Y,X_i) to (Ye^s,X_i+s), and D_i is translation in X_i.

The coefficient of b^α in f_l∘Φ is (D_B^α f_l)|_Z/α!. By (2.1), every coefficient with ∑κ_{B_i}α_i<εN is identically zero as a function on the local Z.

Let S be the affine coordinate slice obtained by fixing x_i for i∉A. Its tangent space complements T_Z. Thus b restricted to S has invertible differential and is a holomorphic coordinate system on S; write z=z(b) there. The surviving expansion is

    f_l|_S=∑_{∑κ_{B_i}α_i≥εN} c_{l,α}(z(b))b^α.

Each substituted coefficient has a power series with nonnegative exponents. It cannot lower the weight already carried by b^α. Hence the completed restricted ideal lies in the monomial ideal of B-weight at least εN. This is a particular one-sided containment, not invariance of weighted order under arbitrary coordinate changes.

The restricted ideal has finite colength in the algebraic local ring of S. Here is an algebraic justification of this point. In the ambient local ring, the radical of the original ideal is the ideal P of the selected smooth Z, since the point lies on no other component. If J_S is the ideal of the slice, transversality says the differentials of P+J_S span the full cotangent space. Thus P+J_S plus the square of the maximal ideal is the maximal ideal; Nakayama gives P+J_S equal to the maximal ideal. Taking radicals of the original ideal plus J_S gives the same maximal ideal. After passing to S, the restricted ideal I is therefore maximal-primary.

#### Proof: constant combinations without a reduction theorem

Let R be this k-dimensional regular local ring, with maximal ideal m, and write I=(f₁|_S,…,f_r|_S). There are k constant complex linear combinations g₁,…,g_k for which J=(g₁,…,g_k) is m-primary.

To prove this, at stage j consider the finitely many minimal primes P of J_j=(g₁,…,g_j) with positive-dimensional quotient. They are not m. Since I is m-primary, I is not contained in any such P. The vectors λ∈C^r for which ∑λ_i f_i|_S∈P form a proper linear subspace. Avoid their finite union; also avoid the kernel of C^r→R to make the combination nonzero. A finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot exhaust C^r.

Every prime Q minimal over J_{j+1} contains a minimal prime P over J_j. If P=m then Q=m. Otherwise the choice gives g_{j+1}∉P, so P⊊Q. Prepending P to a chain beginning at Q gives dim(R/Q)≤dim(R/P)−1. Thus the maximum positive dimension drops at least one at every stage. After k stages R/J has dimension zero and J⊂I⊂m, so J is m-primary. In particular,

    length(R/I)≤length(R/J).                               (2.3)

All g_i have the same weighted-degree bound N because the combinations have constant coefficients. No Hilbert–Samuel reduction, equidimensionality, or catenarity assertion is used.

#### Proof: the lower length bound and completion

Because I is m-primary, m^e⊂I for some e. This can be seen directly by taking a power of each local parameter in I and then using the pigeonhole principle for monomials. Thus R/I and its completion have the same finite complex dimension: every formal series reduces to a polynomial of degree below e. For injectivity, truncate formal coefficients in an expression r=∑h_i f_i modulo the e-th power of the maximal ideal; the remainder lies in m^e⊂I. A rational germ with unit denominator has the same formal order as its numerator, so this computation also holds in the localized polynomial ring R.

The holomorphic coordinate changes induce isomorphisms of completed power-series rings and preserve this finite dimension. The preceding weighted ideal containment therefore yields

    length(R/I)≥#{α∈N^k:∑κ_{B_i}α_i<εN}
               ≥(εN)^k/(k!∏_{b∈B}κ_b).                  (2.4)

For the second inequality, cover the open weighted simplex in R_{≥0}^k by the disjoint unit cubes indexed by the coordinatewise floor. Every such cube index satisfies the same strict weight bound; the simplex has the displayed volume.

#### Proof: the upper length bound

Choose an integer M with every Mρ_a a positive integer. Substitute

    x_a=λ_a+s_a^{Mρ_a},    a∈A,

where λ_a differs from the chosen point's x_a coordinate. There are exactly ∏_{a∈A}(Mρ_a) preimages of the point, all unramified. At each, the local analytic inverse theorem identifies the completed quotient by the substituted g_i with the original quotient R/J.

Expanding a translated monomial can only decrease its coordinatewise exponents. Each substituted g_i consequently has ordinary degree at most MN. The multiplicity-counted isolated-zero form of affine Bézout bounds the total local length at these preimages by (MN)^k, even if there are positive-dimensional components elsewhere. Hence

    length(R/J)≤N^k/∏_{a∈A}ρ_a.                            (2.5)

Combining (2.3)–(2.5) proves (2.2). The Bézout statement and its local quotient convention are specified in Section 9. ∎

## 3. The algebraic obstruction to an exact logarithmic differential

### Lemma 3.1

Let K be a finitely generated extension of C, and x,y∈K with y≠0. If dx=dy/y in Ω_{K/C}, then y∈C.

#### Proof

Suppose y is nonconstant. Since C is algebraically closed, y is transcendental over C. Extend y to a transcendence basis y,z₂,…,z_s. Put L=C(z₂,…,z_s), F=L(y); then K/F is finite separable, of degree e≥1.

The derivation ∂/∂y on F extends uniquely to K. For an algebraic generator this follows by differentiating its separable minimal polynomial, whose derivative is nonzero. The differential identity gives ∂x=1/y.

Trace commutes with the derivation: embed K in a normal closure, express trace as the sum over distinct F-embeddings, and use uniqueness of the extended derivation to commute it with each embedding. Therefore

    ∂(Tr_{K/F}x)=e/y.

The trace is a rational function of y over L. In its Laurent expansion at y=0, a derivative has zero coefficient of y^{−1}; that coefficient would have to arise by differentiating a constant. The right side has the nonzero coefficient e. Contradiction. ∎

This proof is algebraic. It requires neither choosing a curve section of a higher-dimensional variety nor an assertion about residues after such a choice.

## 4. A weighted curve inequality

For an irreducible affine algebraic curve C, use its smooth projective model and the induced rational coordinate functions. Define

    d_W(C)=∑_P max(0,−ord_P(Y)/w₀,
                          −ord_P(X₁)/w₁,…,−ord_P(X_m)/w_m).

An identically zero coordinate contributes no pole. For a branch P through a_j, define

    h_P=min(ord_P(Y/y_j−1)/v₀,
            min_i ord_P(X_i−c_ji−log(Y/y_j))/v_i).          (4.1)

The logarithm in (4.1) is expanded formally at Y/y_j=1. Each term is positive or infinite. The minimum is finite: if Y/y_j−1 and every additive formal coordinate vanished identically on the branch, all original coordinates would be constant in the curve's function field.

### Proposition 4.1

With the weights chosen in Section 1, every such curve satisfies

    d_W(C)≥(1+σ)∑_{P above a center}h_P.                  (4.2)

#### Proof: auxiliary polynomial and its derivatives

If C meets no center, the right side is zero and the assertion is immediate. Otherwise suppose (4.2) fails. Write d=m+1 and S_V=∑v_i. The number of source monomials of W-degree at most N is at least N^d/(d!∏W). The number of coefficients of V-weight less than (1+3σ)N at all centers is at most

    K((1+3σ)N+S_V)^d/(d!∏V).

The first bound covers a simplex by admissible unit cubes; the second places the cubes attached to admissible indices inside the enlarged simplex. By (1.3), the first number exceeds the second whenever

    N>S_V/(λ^{−1/d}−(1+3σ)).                               (4.3)

Thus there is a nonzero polynomial F of W-degree at most N with V-order at least (1+3σ)N at every center. This is only a homogeneous linear-system count and does not presuppose interpolation.

On U={Y≠0}, set

    D₀=Y∂Y+∑_{i>0}∂Xi,       D_i=∂Xi.

These commuting fields form a frame and do not increase W-degree. In the local logarithmic coordinates at any center,

    D₀=(1+t)∂t,       D_i=∂u_i.

They decrease V-order by at most their respective v_i. Hence every D^γF of cost ∑v_iγ_i≤σN has V-order at least (1+2σ)N at every center. Substitution along a branch P gives zero order at least (1+2σ)N h_P, unless that rational function is identically zero.

Every polynomial of W-degree at most N has total pole degree on the complete smooth model of C at most N d_W(C), by applying the monomial weight budget at each pole. Since (4.2) is assumed false, the specified total zero order is strictly larger than this total pole bound. A nonzero rational function on a proper smooth curve has equal total zero and pole degree. It follows that

    D^γF vanishes identically on C whenever ∑v_iγ_i≤σN.    (4.4)

#### Proof: a repeated component and its normal bases

Set δ=σN/L₀. For r=0,…,L₀, let Z_r be the common zero set in U of derivatives of cost at most rδ. It is proper because its defining equations include the nonzero F, and it contains the dense C∩U by (4.4).

Choose an irreducible component of Z_{L₀} containing C∩U, then successively a component of each preceding Z_r containing the already selected component. Such a component exists because an irreducible set contained in a finite union of closed sets lies in one of them. This is a nested chain, all of whose dimensions are between 1 and m. Two adjacent members have the same dimension, and therefore coincide. Denote this component by Z, of codimension k∈{1,…,m}.

Use the earlier level's derivative polynomials as equations for Lemma 2.1. Any further derivative of cost <δ belongs to the next level and vanishes on Z. The lemma with ε=σ/L₀ yields, for every coordinate normal basis A and D-normal basis B,

    ∏_{a∈A}w_a≤C*∏_{b∈B}v_b.                             (4.5)

We claim that Y is constant on Z. If some ∂Xi with i>0 is generically nontangent to Z, choose the largest such i and a coordinate normal basis A containing i. All positive fields of larger index are tangent and occur in no normal basis in either frame. If a D-normal basis B omitted i, (1.5) would contradict (4.5). Thus every D-normal basis contains D_i.

At a generic smooth point, let V_t be the ambient tangent space and T=T_Z. The other D-fields span the hyperplane

    H=ker(dXi−dY/Y).

Their images do not span V_t/T. Equivalently H+T is proper in V_t. Since H already has codimension one, this means T⊂H. Therefore dXi=dY/Y on the function field of Z. Lemma 3.1 makes Y constant.

If every positive coordinate field is tangent, their m-dimensional span is contained in T. Properness of Z gives dim T≤m, so T is exactly that span, and dY=0. A nonconstant rational function in characteristic zero can be included in a transcendence basis and has a derivation taking value one, so dY=0 likewise forces Y constant.

Since C⊂Z, Y is constant on C. Distinctness of the y_j leaves at most one center on C. Choose a nonconstant coordinate X_l on C. On branches above that center, log(Y/y_j)=0. Thus X_l−c_jl has total zero order at least v_l∑h_P, and total pole degree at most w_l d_W(C). Divisor degree equality gives

    d_W(C)≥(v_l/w_l)∑h_P=θ^{−1}∑h_P
             >(1+σ)∑h_P,

contradicting the assumed failure of (4.2). ∎

No component is required to be independent of N, and no transverse-coordinate distinctness is used. The only use of distinct y_j is in the last paragraph.

## 5. Ordinary powers on a pure-power blowup

The precise center ideals needed later admit the following direct calculation.

### Lemma 5.1

Let A be a commutative ring, R=A[z₁,…,z_d], and I=(z₁^{a₁},…,z_d^{a_d}), with positive integers a_i. Let

    p:B=Proj(⊕_{n≥0}IⁿTⁿ)→Spec R

be the ordinary, unnormalized blowup. Write O_B(1)=I O_B=O_B(−E). For every n≥0,

    p_*O_B(−nE)=Iⁿ,       R^q p_*O_B(−nE)=0 for q>0.       (5.1)

#### Proof

Put f_i=z_i^{a_i}. The standard affine charts U_i=D₊(f_iT) cover B, and every finite intersection U_S is affine. Suppressing the common T-degree n, the sections of O_B(n) are monomial A-submodules of a Laurent polynomial ring. A Laurent monomial z^b, b∈Z^d, occurs on U_S exactly when

    b_j≥0 for j∉S,       ∑_i floor(b_i/a_i)≥n.              (5.2)

Indeed, a monomial in the localized Rees algebra of T-degree n has form z^r∏f_i^{e_i}, with r_i≥0, ∑e_i=n, e_j≥0 outside S and arbitrary integral e_i inside S. This is equivalent to having e_i≤floor(b_i/a_i). Condition (5.2) is necessary. For sufficiency start with e_i=floor(b_i/a_i) and decrease one e_i with i∈S by the surplus in their sum.

The finite affine-intersection Čech complex computes sheaf cohomology. It splits by Laurent exponent b. If the sum in (5.2) is less than n, the b-complex is zero. Otherwise put T_b={i:b_i<0}. Its terms are copies of A indexed by nonempty S⊇T_b, in degree |S|−1, with the ordinary alternating coboundary.

If T_b is empty, this is a simplex complex, giving A in H⁰ and zero in higher degrees. If T_b is a nonempty proper subset, choose v∉T_b. The usual simplex contraction inserts v. Explicitly, for an increasingly ordered S,

    (hc)_S=0 if v∈S;
    (hc)_S=(−1)^r c_{S∪{v}} otherwise,

where r is the zero-based position of v in S∪{v}. Removing v does not remove any element of T_b, so this is a well-defined contraction within the subcomplex, with dh+hd=1. If T_b is the full index set, its only term is in degree d−1; but then every floor(b_i/a_i)≤−1, so their sum is ≤−d<n. This case is excluded for n≥0.

Thus all higher cohomology vanishes. Surviving H⁰ monomials satisfy b_i≥0 and ∑floor(b_i/a_i)≥n, which is exactly the divisibility criterion for membership in Iⁿ. All decompositions are direct sums of free A-modules and all homotopies use 0,1,−1, so the computation is valid over A. Localizing the finite complex at any base-ring element is exact. It proves the sheaf statements in (5.1), not merely a statement about global sections. ∎

This lemma is not an all-n assertion for arbitrary ideals. The pure-power form is essential to the displayed proof.

## 6. Compactification and algebraic center ideals

Choose an integer R with every R/w_i and R/v_i a positive integer. Embed affine (m+1)-space using all W-monomials of degree at most R, including 1, and let X be its integral projective closure. Every coordinate is among the monomials. The chart where the constant coordinate is nonzero is therefore precisely the original affine space: the affine coordinate algebra generated by the listed monomials contains all original variables and is their polynomial ring. Let L=O_X(1).

For a curve C meeting this chart,

    deg_C L=R d_W(C).                                      (6.1)

To see this, pull back to its smooth complete model. At a point P the largest pole among the monomial sections equals R times the maximum in the definition of d_W: the upper bound follows from the weight budget, and equality is attained by the pure power x_i^{R/w_i} of a coordinate realizing the maximum, or by 1. Clearing these maximal poles gives a linear series with no base points, so its divisor degree is exactly the degree of the pullback of O(1).

At center j choose, for each i>0, a Taylor polynomial G_i(t) of log(1+t) whose omitted terms have t-weight strictly greater than v_i. For example take degree e_i with (e_i+1)v₀>v_i. Set

    t=Y/y_j−1,       u_i'=X_i−c_ji−G_i(t).

This is a polynomial coordinate change on the affine chart, with polynomial inverse Y=y_j(1+t), X_i=c_ji+u_i'+G_i(t). The difference u_i'−u_i from the formal logarithmic coordinate has V-weight >v_i. Substitution in either direction preserves each ideal M_V(H), since replacing a variable by itself plus higher-weight terms cannot lower a monomial's weight.

It preserves the branch minimum h_P as well. The correction has order >(v_i/v₀)ord_P(t)≥v_i h_P. Therefore it cannot lower that minimum. Applying the inverse substitution proves equality.

At j take the ideal

    I_j=(t^{R/v₀},(u₁')^{R/v₁},…,(u_m')^{R/v_m}).          (6.2)

It has finite support consisting only of a_j. These ideals, with the unit ideal away from the centers, glue to a coherent ideal I on X: on the affine chart one can take their product, and on the complement of their finite support it is the unit ideal.

Let p:X'→X be the ordinary blowup of I, and write

    I O_{X'}=O_{X'}(−E),       A=p*L.

The blowup is projective; E is effective Cartier; and −E is relatively ample. X' is integral: locally its affine blowup charts are subrings of the fraction field of the integral affine coordinate ring. In neighborhoods of the centers, (6.2) is exactly a pure-power ideal after a polynomial coordinate change. Lemma 5.1 applies there. Outside the centers p is the identity. Its conclusions glue to

    p_*O_{X'}(−nE)=Iⁿ,       R^q p_*O_{X'}(−nE)=0          (6.3)

for all n≥0 and q>0. This proves ordinary-power recovery directly for the actual ideals, without a normalized blowup or any eventual recovery theorem.

## 7. From curve separation to an ample divisor

Let Γ be an irreducible curve in X'. If Γ is not contracted, its generic point lies outside the exceptional center, because that center is finite. Therefore Γ is the strict transform of its image and is birational to it. If its image meets a center, their common normalized function field gives

    A·Γ=R d_W(pΓ),       E·Γ=R∑_P h_P.                    (7.1)

For the second formula, the ideal (6.2) in each discrete valuation ring has order equal to the minimum order of its generators, namely R h_P. Thus Proposition 4.1 shows that A−(1+σ)E has nonnegative degree on Γ.

If a noncontracted image avoids the centers, then E·Γ=0 and A·Γ≥0. This includes curves entirely in the boundary of the affine chart. For a contracted curve, A·Γ=0 and (−E)·Γ>0 by relative ampleness. These cases exhaust all curves, so

    A−(1+σ)E is nef.                                       (7.2)

For some a>1, aA−E is ample. More explicitly choose b≥1 with I⊗L^b globally generated. Its generating sections give the closed Rees immersion

    X' ↪ X×P^r,

where the pullback of the second O(1) is bA−E. The restriction of the very ample external tensor product L⊠O(1) is (b+1)A−E. Take a=b+1.

Now the exact identity

    A−E = [(a−1)/(a(1+σ)−1)](A−(1+σ)E)
          + [σ/(a(1+σ)−1)](aA−E)                         (7.3)

has positive coefficients. The first summand is nef and the second a positive multiple of an ample divisor. The nef-plus-ample theorem for projective schemes makes A−E ample. No smoothness or normality of X or X' is required in that theorem.

## 8. Surjective jets and completion of the proof

For sufficiently large n, Serre vanishing for the ample invertible sheaf O_{X'}(A−E) gives

    H¹(X',O_{X'}(n(A−E)))=0.

Using (6.3), projection formula and Leray identifies this group with H¹(X,Iⁿ⊗Lⁿ). The exact sequence for Iⁿ consequently gives a surjection

    H⁰(X,Lⁿ) → H⁰(X,(O_X/Iⁿ)⊗Lⁿ).                        (8.1)

The constant embedding section trivializes L on the affine chart. The right side is therefore the direct sum of the finite local quotients O_{X,a_j}/I_jⁿ, with that fixed trivialization.

Every generator of I_j has V-weight R in the primed variables, so

    I_jⁿ ⊂ M_V(nR).

The resulting map O/I_jⁿ→O/M_V(nR) is surjective. Local, polynomial, and completed versions of these finite quotients agree: the quotient by M_V(nR) is killed by a sufficiently large power of the maximal ideal and every class is represented by its finite polynomial truncation. The triangular parameter change in Section 6 identifies the last quotient with the original logarithmic packet J_V(nR).

Finally, for all sufficiently large n, restriction of degree-n homogeneous polynomials in the projective embedding coordinates onto H⁰(X,Lⁿ) is surjective. This is Serre vanishing for the coherent projective ideal sheaf of X. Each such homogeneous polynomial restricts on the affine chart to a polynomial of W-degree at most nR, since every embedding coordinate has W-degree at most R. Composing this restriction with (8.1) and the finite quotient maps gives exactly (1.2). This proves Theorem A. ∎

Every reduced irreducible component used here is locally algebraic over the perfect field C, so it has a dense smooth open. Normal-basis conditions are nonvanishing determinant conditions on that smooth locus, and the finitely many other irreducible components remove only a proper closed set. In the slice argument the ambient local ring is Noetherian; its maximal ideal and its quotient by P+J_S are finite modules. ### E8. Local analytic foundations

Only formal inverses are ultimately needed. They can also be constructed directly: if a power-series map has zero constant term and invertible linear part L, start its inverse with L^{-1}y. At each homogeneous degree n≥2, the still unknown degree-n correction occurs through L times that correction; the terms determined at smaller degrees are already known.
